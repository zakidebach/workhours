How to calculate your pay

Your gross pay is what you earn before taxes and other deductions. For hourly work, the formula is simple:

Gross pay = Hourly rate × Hours worked
+ Overtime hours × Overtime rate (if any)

Example: You earn $20.00 an hour and work 40 regular hours plus 5 overtime hours in a week. Regular pay is 40 × $20.00 = $800.00. At time and a half, the overtime rate is $30.00, so overtime pay is 5 × $30.00 = $150.00. Gross weekly pay: $950.00.

Monthly pay is not simply four weeks of pay. A year has 52 weeks, so an average month has about 4.33 weeks. Multiplying weekly pay by 4 understates your monthly income by roughly 8%.

Converting pay between minutes, hours, days, weeks, months and years

Choose Convert a pay rate at the top of this page, enter what you earn and the unit it is paid in, and the calculator shows the same pay per minute, hour, day, week, month and year. It works through the yearly total, using the work schedule you set:

Hours per year = Hours per day × Days per week × Weeks per year
Per year = Pay × (number of that unit in a year)
Per month = Per year ÷ 12   (a month = 52 ÷ 12 ≈ 4.33 weeks)
Per minute = Per hour ÷ 60

Example: $20.00 per hour with 8 hours a day, 5 days a week and 52 weeks a year (2,080 hours) equals about $0.33 per minute, $160.00 per day, $800.00 per week, $3,466.67 per month and $41,600.00 per year.

Results are estimates: they assume you work exactly that schedule every week of the year, and they don't include unpaid leave, overtime, bonuses, taxes or deductions. Change the schedule if you work part-time, have fewer working weeks, or work longer days. The currency you pick only changes how amounts are shown; nothing is converted between currencies.

About overtime

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), most hourly employees who are non-exempt must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. However, the rule does not apply to everyone:

  • Some employees are exempt from overtime, such as many salaried managers and professionals who meet specific tests.
  • Federal law looks at the workweek, not the day. Working more than 8 hours in one day does not by itself require overtime under federal law.
  • State laws can be more generous. California, for example, requires daily overtime in many cases.
  • Your employer or contract may pay overtime differently.

That's why the 1.5× option is off by default. Turn it on only if it matches your situation, and treat the result as an estimate.

Gross pay vs. take-home pay

This calculator shows gross pay only. It does not subtract federal, state or local taxes, Social Security, Medicare, insurance or retirement contributions, because those depend on your personal situation. Your paycheck will be lower than the gross amount. To plan your monthly spending, enter your take-home pay in the Salary Breakdown tool, or enter your gross pay and add your taxes as a category.
